ICT324 · Agriculture & Climate Resilience

Methods & evidence

Research evidence reviewed 13 November 2026. This guide reports saved results; it is not a new experiment or an operational prediction service.

The unchanged question

How do unusually high temperatures and low rainfall relate to crop yield variability, and which selected crops exhibit the greatest yield stability?

Data and preparation

Five OWID exports contain 67,299 source-table rows. Cleaning retains 23,412 crop records; 752 lack complete weather, leaving 22,660 unique country/year/crop observations across 173 countries/territories. Climate has a separate union of 12,545 country-years. The five-country Southeast Asian subset preserves all 743 original crop records. Missing national inputs remain missing.

Production code checks schema/units, numeric values, current ISO3 identifiers and integer years, records duplicate/missing-value checks, retains genuine zeros and diagnostic outliers, validates join cardinality and traces retained measurements to saved sources. A correct country-year join does not establish crop-season representativeness or upstream measurement accuracy.

Different targets, compatible comparisons

  • M3: 100 × (yield − earlier full-history expected yield) / expected yield, with expected yield >0.1 t/ha. Climate reference 1961–1990; at least 20 reference observations. Training 1961–2015, 18,914 eligible rows; original final test 2016–2024, 3,370 rows.
  • V2/V3: common evaluation target 100 × (yield − earlier full-history trend) / S, with S=max(0.5 t/ha, earlier country/crop median yield). The model corrects a prior yield reference. Require at least 20 full-history, 15 recent-20-year and three previous-five-year yield observations. The statistical floor changes normalization, not measured yields.
  • Chronology: train through 1995/2002/2009, validate 1996–2002/2003–2009/2010–2015. Forward calibration residuals use references fitted before each outcome block. Outer references stay fixed through the validation block; scalers/encoders learn on training rows only.
  • Later studies: 2016–2024 is excluded from model development and new candidate evaluation. Previously examined historical windows remain exploratory; V3 reproducing V2 is not an independent holdout.

Association and stability evidence

V3 correlations are descriptive. Adjusted regressions include country/year controls and temperature, precipitation and their product. Saved country-cluster and separate year-cluster intervals include zero for all climate terms. These methods do not establish causal effects.

Primary stability uses 61 countries and 1,219 common country-years per crop, first calculating within-country sample SD then equally weighting countries. Mean SD is rice 21.9513, wheat 26.5458 and maize 34.9142 historical-scale points. The wheat-minus-rice interval −1.7503 to +11.9351 includes zero. Combined-event support is too sparse for a universal stress-resilience ranking. Hot is ≥+1°C and dry is ≤−20% relative to 1961–1990. Exclusive categories avoid double-counting; threshold/reference sensitivities remain important.

Regional national inputs

303 fertilizer and 18 irrigation country-years were retrieved through OWID. Fertilizer matches 707 of the 743 crop rows and 611 of 635 pre-2016 rows. Irrigation matches 41 overall and 12 historical crop rows; the latter represent six Laos years, so irrigation is excluded from modeling. FAOSTAT RFN discovery returned401; direct World Bank requests failed. Those failed direct requests are not counted as successful datasets.

National fertilizer per hectare of cropland differs from the originally attempted World Bank arable-land denominator. Neither fertilizer nor irrigation is crop-specific. Source revisions and unknown historical publication dates prevent assuming advance availability.

Settings and interpretation

M3 selected a depth-3/leaf-40 tree and depth-6/leaf-20 forest (100 trees). V3 uses 80 trees, depth6, leaf20, max_features0.8 and seed324. Regional small-sample Ridge uses alpha10 and SVD; tree and forest use depth3/leaf5/min split10, with 80 forest trees and all predictors considered per split. Full one-hot categories and training-only numerical scaling are used for the new small-sample models. Fixed seeds325/326 are sensitivities, not selected alternative models.

The early regional forest's one-leaf limitation is resolved, but extra capacity does not guarantee improved validation. Ridge/fertilizer has the lowest observed average, while its incremental fertilizer benefit depends heavily on Vietnam and the earliest period. Its wheat errors worsen. Country/crop-only Ridge is a provisional rice/maize benchmark, not an adopted global method.

Some settings and category encoding differ across experiments. Multiple comparisons, reused validation, sparse event groups, repeated national exposures and coarse annual climate limit interpretation. None of these models demonstrates a causal fertilizer/climate response or an operational before-harvest forecast.
